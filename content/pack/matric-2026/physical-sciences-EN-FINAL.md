# PHYSICAL SCIENCES — PRELIM STRATEGY SHEET

## 1. THE PAPER AT A GLANCE

Physical Sciences is really two subjects wearing one name — and the exam treats it that way.

| | Paper 1 — Physics | Paper 2 — Chemistry |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **Question 1** | 10 multiple-choice questions — 20 marks | 10 multiple-choice questions — 20 marks |
| **The rest** | Structured questions — 130 marks | Structured questions — 130 marks |

**Information sheets are provided for both papers** — formulas, constants, the periodic table. You never memorise a formula in this subject; you train *choosing and using* the right one.

---

## 2. WHERE THE MARKS LIVE

**Paper 1 — Physics (150 marks):**

| Topic | Marks |
|---|---|
| Mechanics (Newton's laws, momentum, work-energy-power, projectile motion) | 65 |
| Electricity & magnetism (electrostatics, circuits, electrodynamics) | 55 |
| Waves, sound & light (Doppler effect) | 15 |
| Matter & materials (photoelectric effect) | 15 |

**Paper 2 — Chemistry (150 marks):**

| Topic | Marks |
|---|---|
| Chemical change (rates, equilibrium, acids & bases, electrochemistry) | 92 |
| Matter & materials (organic chemistry & intermolecular forces) | 58 |

**The play:** Mechanics + Electricity = **120 of Physics' 150 marks.** Two territories, eighty percent of the paper. In Chemistry, Chemical Change is nearly two-thirds of the paper — but Organic (inside Matter & Materials at 58 marks) is the most *learnable* chunk: naming, reactions and properties are drilled skills, not talent. Anchor Mechanics, Electricity and Organic first; they're the biggest returns on your hours.

---

## 3. THE EASY MARKS

1. **Question 1 in both papers: 20 marks of multiple choice.** No working needed, no method marks to lose. Do it first, do it calmly, and never leave one blank — a guess scores more than a gap.
2. **Definitions and laws, word for word.** "State Newton's Second Law", "Define the Doppler effect" — 2-mark gifts to learners who memorised the exact wording. Keep a definitions list from Week 1; recite it weekly.
3. **Formula + substitution.** Choose the correct formula from the information sheet, substitute visibly, then solve — the early steps carry marks before the final answer does. Write the formula. Every time.
4. **Organic naming.** IUPAC naming follows rules like a recipe — longest chain, number the substituents, alphabetical order. Drilled for a week, it's free marks for life.
5. **Reading values off given graphs and tables.** Rates graphs, titration curves, circuit diagrams — the first sub-question is usually "read this off." Do it carefully, with units.

---

## 4. THE KILLERS

1. **Missing units.** An answer without its unit loses the mark, every time. Newtons, joules, mol·dm⁻³ — write the unit before you feel finished.
2. **Missing direction on vectors.** Momentum, force, velocity — magnitude AND direction. "12 N" when the answer is "12 N east" is half an answer.
3. **No diagram, wrong formula.** Mechanics collapses without a free-body diagram; electricity without redrawing the circuit. The drawing isn't decoration — it chooses your formula for you.
4. **Sign convention chaos.** In momentum and projectile questions: choose your positive direction, write it down, stay loyal to it for the whole question. Learners flip halfway and the marks flip with them.
5. **Unbalanced equations.** Every chemical equation you write gets checked. Balance it before you move on — it's ten seconds that protects every mark downstream.
6. **Equilibrium hand-waving.** "The equilibrium shifts right" without the WHY (Le Chatelier's reasoning, named) earns sympathy, not marks. Statement + reason, always.

---

## 5. YOUR 7-WEEK FOCUS (syncs with your Countdown Planner)

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full P1 OR P2 (your weaker half first), untimed — build your Gap Map. Start your definitions list |
| 2 | Mechanics deep repair: Newton's laws → momentum → work-energy-power. One free-body diagram per day minimum |
| 3 | Organic chemistry: naming, reactions, physical properties — then intermolecular forces. The banker week |
| 4 | Electricity & magnetism + one timed section: circuits daily, then electrodynamics. Definitions recital #1 |
| 5 | Chemical change I: rates + equilibrium (statement + reason drills), then full timed Paper 1 |
| 6 | Chemical change II: acids & bases + electrochemistry, then full Paper 2 simulation. Definitions recital #2 |
| 7 | Taper: error list, definitions final recital, information-sheet fluency — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Audit first. Then Mechanics, Electricity and Organic before anything else — the biggest, most learnable territories. Definitions list every week regardless; it's the cheapest 10+ marks in the subject.

---

## 6. THE FINAL 48 HOURS

**DO:**
- Recite your definitions list out loud — both papers' worth
- Redo your error list, especially sign-convention and unit slips
- One set of 10 old multiple-choice questions per paper, timed, for rhythm
- Check calculator + know where everything lives on the information sheet
- Sleep 8 hours — Physics punishes tired sign errors brutally

**DON'T:**
- Don't learn a new topic the night before (photoelectric effect at midnight = panic, not marks)
- Don't memorise formulas that are on the information sheet — learn to find them fast instead
- Don't practise only Physics because it's "harder" — Chemistry's 150 marks count exactly the same
- Don't skip breakfast before a 3-hour paper
