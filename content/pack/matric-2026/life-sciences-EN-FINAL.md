## 1. THE PAPER AT A GLANCE

Life Sciences rewards precise vocabulary and clear diagrams over raw memorisation — the students who do best learn to *write like the memo*. Two papers, split by topic.

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 2.5 hours | 2.5 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **The territory** | Human Reproduction, Responding to the Environment (humans & plants), Endocrine System & Homeostasis, Reproduction in Vertebrates | DNA: The Code of Life, Meiosis, Genetics & Inheritance, Evolution |

**Both papers share the same structure:**

| Section | What | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Short objective — MCQ, terminology, matching columns/statements | 50 |
| **B** | Two questions of 50 marks each, divided into subquestions | 100 |

---

## 2. WHERE THE MARKS LIVE

**The cognitive split (both papers):**

| Knowledge | Comprehension | Application | Analysis/Synthesis/Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40% | 25% | 20% | 15% |

**40% is pure knowledge** — the single biggest slice, and the most learnable. Terminology, definitions, recall. This is why a strong terminology list is worth more marks in Life Sciences than in almost any other subject.

**Paper 1 mark weightings:**
- Responding to the Environment (humans) — 54 marks
- Endocrine & Homeostasis (humans) — 34 marks
- Human Reproduction — 41 marks
- Responding to the Environment (plants) — 13 marks
- Reproduction in Vertebrates — 8 marks

**Paper 2 mark weightings:**
- **Evolution — 54 marks**
- **Genetics & Inheritance — 48 marks**
- DNA: The Code of Life — 27 marks
- Meiosis — 21 marks

**The play:** In Paper 2, Genetics (48) + Evolution (54) = **102 of 150 marks** — over two-thirds of the paper on two topics. Genetic crosses are pure method (lay out a dihybrid cross cleanly and the marks are yours every year), and evolution answers are preparable in advance. In Paper 1, "Responding to the Environment (humans)" is the single biggest block at 54. Anchor genetics, evolution and the human nervous/eye/ear system first.

## 3. THE EASY MARKS

1. **Section A — 50 marks of objective questions.** MCQ, matching, terminology, "provide the term." No explanation, pure recall — the friendliest 50 marks in the paper. Drill past Section A's.
2. **Terminology.** Life Sciences lives on precise words — meiosis vs mitosis, genotype vs phenotype, artery vs vein. With 40% of marks at knowledge level, your terminology list is your single highest-return study tool. Build it from Week 1.
3. **Labelled diagrams.** The eye, the ear, the neuron, the nephron, a DNA molecule, meiosis phases — marks per correct label. Learn the standard diagrams; the labels are guaranteed marks.
4. **Genetic crosses.** Monohybrid and dihybrid crosses follow a fixed layout — parents, gametes, Punnett square, ratio. Method marks at every step. Drill until automatic.
5. **"Tabulate the difference" questions.** Comparing two structures or processes in a table is a common, markable format — learn the classic comparisons.

---

## 4. THE KILLERS

1. **Vague, non-scientific language.** "The blood goes to the heart thing" earns nothing. Life Sciences marks precise terminology — write like the memo, not like a conversation.
2. **Unlabelled or messy diagrams.** A beautiful drawing with no labels, or label lines that don't touch the structure, forfeits the marks. Use a ruler for label lines; label everything clearly.
3. **Explaining "what" when asked "why/how."** "Explain how the kidney regulates water" needs the *mechanism* (ADH, collecting duct), not a description of the kidney. Answer the actual verb.
4. **Genetic cross shortcuts.** Skipping the gametes line or the Punnett square to jump to the answer loses the method marks. Show every step, even when you can see the answer.
5. **Weak evolution arguments.** Evolution questions often ask you to explain or argue using a theory (natural selection, evidence). A list of facts without a logical line of reasoning leaves the higher-order marks behind.
6. **Full sentences, not point form.** Paragraph-style questions expect full sentences — one-word or bullet answers lose marks even when the facts are right.

## 5. YOUR 7-WEEK FOCUS (syncs with your Countdown Planner)

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full P1 AND P2 if you can, untimed — build your Gap Map. Start your terminology list |
| 2 | Genetics (P2): monohybrid & dihybrid crosses — drill the layout until automatic (48 marks of method) |
| 3 | Evolution (P2): theories, evidence, natural selection, speciation — learn the argument structures (54 marks) |
| 4 | P2 DNA & Meiosis + one timed section: protein synthesis, meiosis phases and diagrams |
| 5 | P1 Human systems (nervous, endocrine, homeostasis, eye/ear): mechanisms + labelled diagrams, then full timed Paper 2 |
| 6 | P1 Human reproduction + responding to environment (plants), then full Paper 1 simulation. Rework lost marks |
| 7 | Taper: terminology list, standard diagrams from memory, your error list — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Audit first, then genetics crosses and the standard diagrams — pure method and recall marks you can lock in fast. Terminology list every week regardless: 40% of the paper rewards it.

---

## 6. THE FINAL 48 HOURS

**DO:**
- Recite your terminology list out loud — 40% of marks reward precise words
- Redraw the standard diagrams (eye, neuron, nephron, DNA, meiosis) from memory, labelled
- Do one monohybrid and one dihybrid cross for rhythm
- Redo your error list — especially vague-language and unlabelled-diagram slips
- Sleep 8 hours — clear recall of terminology needs a rested brain

**DON'T:**
- Don't attempt a full new paper the night before
- Don't skip diagram practice — labels are guaranteed marks
- Don't write conversationally; practise the memo's precise phrasing
- Don't skip breakfast before a 2.5-hour paper
