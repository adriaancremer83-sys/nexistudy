# ENGLISH — PRELIM STRATEGY SHEET
### NexiStudy Matric Prelim Survival Pack

---

## 1. THE PAPERS AT A GLANCE

English is three papers testing three different skills — and unlike content subjects, you can't "cram" it. You improve English by *practising the paper types*: reading with a pen, writing to a structure, and knowing your set texts cold. This sheet works for both Home Language (HL) and First Additional Language (FAL) — the papers are the same shape; HL just demands more depth.

| Paper | What it tests | Time | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paper 1** | Comprehension, summary, language & visual literacy (advertisement, cartoon) | 2 hours (HL) / 2 hours (FAL) | 70 (HL) / 80 (FAL) |
| **Paper 2** | Literature — novel, drama, poetry, short stories | 2.5 hours (HL) / 2 hours (FAL) | 80 (HL) / 70 (FAL) |
| **Paper 3** | Writing — one long essay + two shorter texts (transactional) | 2.5 hours (HL) / 2 hours (FAL) | 100 (HL) / 100 (FAL) |

*Check your exact prelim mark allocations against your school's paper — the split above is the standard national format, and HL/FAL differ slightly.*

---

## 2. WHERE THE MARKS LIVE

- **Paper 1** (comprehension & language): the whole paper is technique. You're never asked to recall facts — you're asked to *read carefully and answer precisely*. This is the most improvable paper in a short time because it's pure skill.
- **Paper 2** (literature): you answer only on the texts you've studied — but you MUST know them deeply. Marks reward quoting, context, and analysing *how* a writer creates meaning, not just retelling the plot.
- **Paper 3** (writing): the biggest single paper at 100 marks. Your essay alone is 50, and it's the most preparable thing in the whole subject — because good structure and a few polished techniques work on almost any topic.

**The play:** Paper 3 is where the fastest gains live. A learner who masters one solid essay structure and practises three or four transactional formats (friendly/formal letter, speech, review, obituary, CV) can walk into that paper confident regardless of the topic. Then Paper 1 is trainable through pure practice, and Paper 2 rewards knowing your set works cold.

---

## 3. THE EASY MARKS

1. **Paper 1 "quote/identify/find" questions.** The low-mark comprehension questions ask you to locate the answer in the text. Read the question, find the line, answer precisely — with the exact words when asked to quote.
2. **The summary (Paper 1).** A fixed-format task: pull the required number of points, in full sentences, within the word limit. Follow the format exactly and the marks are procedural, not creative.
3. **Paper 3 format marks.** Every transactional text (letter, speech, review) earns marks for correct *format* — heading, greeting, layout, register — before the content is judged. Learn the formats; the layout marks are free.
4. **Paper 2 "quote in context" questions.** Knowing key quotes from your set works and being able to place them earns direct marks. Build a quote bank per text.
5. **Language questions (Paper 1).** Parts of speech, figures of speech, correcting sentences — rules-based recall marks. Learn the common ones (metaphor, personification, tone).

---

## 4. THE KILLERS

1. **Answering comprehension in your own words when asked to quote (or vice versa).** "Quote" means exact words in quotation marks. "In your own words" means don't quote. Do the wrong one and lose the mark. Read the instruction.
2. **Retelling the plot instead of analysing.** In Paper 2, "Discuss how the writer creates tension" is not "tell me what happens." Answer the *how* — the technique, the effect — with a quote to back it.
3. **Ignoring the word count and format in Paper 3.** An essay wildly over or under length, or a transactional text in the wrong format, loses marks before content. Plan, then write to the brief.
4. **Not planning the essay.** A five-minute plan produces a structured essay; no plan produces a ramble. The intro-body-conclusion shape is worth real marks.
5. **Weak or no evidence in literature answers.** Higher-mark literature questions need quotes and specific references. General impressions don't score at the top.
6. **Running out of time in Paper 3.** One long essay plus two shorter texts is a lot of writing. Budget: essay first (it's worth the most), then the two shorter pieces.

## 5. YOUR 7-WEEK FOCUS (syncs with your Countdown Planner)

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full Paper 1 + one essay, untimed — build your Gap Map. Note which paper leaks most |
| 2 | Paper 3 essay: master ONE flexible structure, write two timed essays on different topics |
| 3 | Paper 3 transactional: drill the formats (formal/friendly letter, speech, review, etc.) |
| 4 | Paper 1 comprehension + summary: practise past papers with a pen — the summary format especially |
| 5 | Paper 2 literature: build quote banks for your set works, practise "how"-style analysis questions |
| 6 | Full timed paper simulations (rotate P1, P2, P3). Rework every lost mark |
| 7 | Taper: essay structures + transactional formats + quote banks from memory — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Paper 3 first — one strong essay structure plus a few formats is the fastest, most reliable improvement. Then Paper 1 practice, which is pure trainable technique.

---

## 6. THE FINAL 48 HOURS

**DO:**
- Review your essay structure and the transactional formats (layout, register)
- Run through your quote banks for the set works
- Do one timed comprehension for reading rhythm
- Redo your error list — especially "quote vs own words" and format slips
- Sleep 8 hours — reading accuracy and clear writing both need a rested brain

**DON'T:**
- Don't try to read a whole set work the night before — revise quotes and themes instead
- Don't skip planning your essays "to save time" — the plan earns marks
- Don't ignore word counts and formats
- Don't skip breakfast before a long writing paper
