# THE PARENTS' GUIDE
### NexiStudy Matric Prelim Survival Pack
**How to help without hovering**

---

## 1. YOUR JOB (AND WHAT ISN'T)

Let's be honest about something first: you cannot write these exams. You probably can't remember how to calculate compound interest, and that is completely fine — because that was never your job.

Your job over the next seven weeks is **structure, fuel and calm**. The plan is on the fridge. The strategy is in the sheets. Nexi and the teachers handle the content. What your child cannot buy, download or Google is a home where the next seven weeks feel *survivable* — and that part is all yours.

Here's the shift that makes everything easier: **stop asking "have you studied?" and start asking "what does the plan say?"** The first question starts a fight. The second one points at the fridge.

---

## 2. HOW THE SEVEN WEEKS WORK (THE 2-MINUTE VERSION)

So you know what's happening when your child disappears into their room:

- **Week 1 is diagnosis, not studying.** They write old exam papers to find out exactly where marks are leaking. If it looks like they're "just doing past papers," that's the plan working.
- **Weeks 2–3 are repair.** Their weakest subjects get the most hours. This is deliberate — marks come from fixing weaknesses, not polishing favourites.
- **Weeks 4–6 turn up the pressure slowly.** Timed sections, then full papers, then full exam simulations under real conditions.
- **Week 7 is taper.** Light revision only, and sleep is protected. If they're not cramming the night before, that is not laziness — that is the plan, and it's the most evidence-backed part of it.

The engine behind it all is the **Gap Map** — one page where every subject is ranked by where marks are bleeding. It gets updated every Sunday. That page is your window into everything, and it's the centre of your Sunday check-in.

---

## 3. THE SUNDAY CHECK-IN (YOUR 15 MINUTES A WEEK)

One structured conversation a week beats seven days of hovering. Every Sunday evening, same time if you can:

**The shape of it:**
1. **Ask to see the Gap Map.** Not their books, not their phone — just the one page. ("Wys my die kaart" / "Show me the map.")
2. **Ask one forward question:** "Which subject gets the double blocks this week?" Let them tell *you* the plan. Saying it out loud to a parent is commitment psychology that works on all humans, including teenagers.
3. **Ask one human question:** "What was the hardest part of this week?" Then listen. Don't fix. Don't lecture. Just listen.
4. **Close with logistics:** anything they need — stationery, a lift, printing, quiet hours, a favourite meal before simulation week.

The exact scripts — copy-paste ready — are in your **Sunday WhatsApp Scripts** bonus. Use them word for word if that's easier; they're written to not start fights.

**What NOT to say on a Sunday (or any day):**
- "In my time we didn't have all these apps and we managed." (True. Also useless.)
- "Your cousin got seven distinctions." (Comparison is fuel for exactly nothing.)
- "You'd better pass, this is costing me money." (They know. Pressure doesn't add marks; it subtracts sleep.)

---

## 4. THE DAILY SUPPORT THAT ACTUALLY HELPS

None of this requires you to understand a single exam question:

- **Protect the study blocks.** During a 50-minute block: no chores, no "quickly come help me," no visitors sent to their room. Their phone should be *out of the room* — back the plan up on this one, it's the single biggest mark-saver in the pack.
- **Feed the machine.** Regular meals, water within reach, fruit that can be grabbed. Simulation days (Week 6) get a proper breakfast — they're training for three-hour papers.
- **Protect the rest day.** One full day off per week is IN the plan. If you see them resting on that day, they are not slacking — they are following instructions. Say nothing except maybe "enjoy it."
- **Guard the sleep, especially Week 7.** A learner who sleeps 8 hours outperforms one who crams till 02:00 — this isn't opinion, it's one of the most consistent findings in learning research. In taper week, lights-out is an act of love.
- **Keep the house calm.** Seven weeks is long. Big family dramas, renovations and shouting matches all have a marks column, even if nobody writes it down.

---

## 5. WARNING SIGNS — AND WHAT TO DO

Some pressure is normal. These signs are not:

- **Avoidance dressed as busyness** — hours "studying" but the Gap Map never changes, or they can't tell you what they worked on. Usually means one subject scares them so much they're orbiting it. *What helps:* name it gently. "It looks like Physical Sciences is the scary one. What would make it less scary this week — Nexi, a classmate, asking the teacher?"
- **The wall of tears or the short fuse** — everything is suddenly a fight, or small things cause big tears. *What helps:* lower the temperature, not the standards. "One block tonight, then we watch something. Tomorrow we go again."
- **Not sleeping** — awake past midnight regularly, exhausted by morning. *What helps:* enforce a hard stop time. Marks made after 23:00 are borrowed from tomorrow, with interest.
- **"It doesn't matter anyway"** — hopelessness talk, giving up language, saying the prelims or their future is pointless. *What helps:* take it seriously and don't debate the logic of it. Ask, listen, and stay close. If that kind of talk continues, or your gut says something is genuinely wrong, bring in help — the school's counsellor, your GP, or a professional. That is never an overreaction; that is a parent doing the job properly.

The line to remember: **be the calm, not the extra storm.** Your child will borrow your nervous system for the next seven weeks. Lend them a steady one.

---

## 6. THE LAST 48 HOURS BEFORE EACH PAPER — PARENT EDITION

- **Two nights before:** normal routine, normal supper, early-ish night. Nothing special — special creates pressure.
- **The night before:** they do light revision only (their Strategy Sheet explains what). Your jobs: a decent meal, the exam kit check (calculator batteries, pens, admission details, water bottle), and lights out at a sane hour. No new topics, no "are you ready?", no pep talk longer than one sentence.
- **The morning of:** breakfast, on time, and one line as they leave. Not "good luck with the whole future" — something like: **"Go collect what you've earned."**
- **After the paper:** do NOT do a post-mortem at the front door. "How was it?" — then accept whatever answer comes, including a grunt. The next paper matters more than the autopsy of the last one. If it went badly, tonight is for supper and sleep; the Gap Map can hear about it on Sunday.

---

## 7. THE ONE-PAGE SUMMARY (STICK THIS NEXT TO THE PLANNER)

**DO:** ask to see the Gap Map on Sundays · protect study blocks and the rest day · feed them properly · guard sleep in Week 7 · keep the house calm · say "go collect what you've earned"

**DON'T:** ask "have you studied?" ten times a day · compare them to cousins · threaten · do front-door post-mortems · panic in front of them · treat the rest day as laziness

Seven weeks. You've got the easier half of the job — and the half that matters most.
