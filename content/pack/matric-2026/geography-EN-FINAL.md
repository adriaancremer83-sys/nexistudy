# GEOGRAPHY — PRELIM STRATEGY SHEET
### NexiStudy Matric Prelim Survival Pack

---

## 1. THE PAPER AT A GLANCE

Geography splits neatly into theory you can learn and mapwork skills you can drill — and because the mapwork calculations are the *same every year*, it's one of the most targetable subjects for banking marks. Two papers, written on separate days.

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **Section A (theory)** | Climate & Weather + Geomorphology | Settlement + Economic Geography of SA |
| **Section B (skills)** | Geographical Skills & Techniques (mapwork) | Geographical Skills & Techniques (mapwork) |

**Both papers, same structure:** Section A = TWO questions of 60 marks each (theory). Section B = ONE question of 30 marks (mapwork/GIS). **All three questions are compulsory** — there's no choice, so you must prepare everything.

---

## 2. WHERE THE MARKS LIVE

**The cognitive split (both papers):**

| Low order | Middle order | High order |
|---|---|---|
| 25% | 50% | 25% |

Half of every paper is middle order — applying knowledge to a scenario, synoptic map, or photograph, not just reciting it.

**Paper 1 theory:** Climate & Weather (mid-latitude cyclones, tropical cyclones, synoptic weather maps) + Geomorphology (fluvial processes, drainage, slopes).
**Paper 2 theory:** Settlement (rural & urban, site & situation, urban issues) + Economic Geography of SA (agriculture, mining, industry, informal sector).
**Both papers, Section B:** the mapwork question — map calculations, cross-sections, GIS, interpreting the 1:50 000 topographic map and orthophoto.

**The play:** Section B mapwork is 30 marks in EACH paper — 60 marks across the two — and the calculation methods (distance, area, gradient, magnetic declination, vertical exaggeration) are **identical every single year**. Drill the six calculations until they're automatic and you've locked in the most predictable marks in the entire subject. This is where organised students separate from crammers.

---

## 3. THE EASY MARKS

1. **The mapwork calculations.** Distance, area, average gradient, vertical exaggeration, magnetic declination, magnetic bearing — each is a fixed formula with method marks along the way. The memo awards marks for measurement, substitution AND answer, so even a wrong final figure earns most of the marks if your working shows. Learn the six cold.
2. **Marks for the formula and substitution.** If the formula isn't given, you earn a mark just for writing the correct one, then more for substituting correctly — before the answer.
3. **Reading synoptic weather maps.** Identifying cold fronts, the South Atlantic High, cyclone stages — recognition marks that come up every year. Learn the symbols.
4. **Definitions (concept, not verbatim).** Marked on the concept, not word-for-word — so you don't need the textbook's exact phrasing, just the correct idea. (1×2) = 2 marks each.
5. **"Identify from the map/photo" questions.** Land use, settlement pattern, river features — the answer is on the resource; you just read it correctly.

---

## 4. THE KILLERS

1. **Point-form answers to paragraph questions.** Middle and higher-order paragraph questions MUST be full sentences — the memo explicitly penalises point form. Write in sentences.
2. **Skipping units in calculations.** Distance in km, area in km², gradient as a ratio, vertical exaggeration as "X times." A right number with the wrong or missing unit loses the final mark.
3. **Rounding or measuring sloppily on the map.** Mapwork rewards precision — measure carefully with your ruler, convert with the scale correctly, and keep decimals until the final step.
4. **Ignoring half a "discuss" or "explain" question.** These need development, not a one-liner. The scenario or map must be *used* in the answer.
5. **Under-preparing one topic because there's no choice.** All three questions are compulsory — you can't dodge geomorphology or economic geography. A gap in your prep is a guaranteed lost block.
6. **Running the clock on theory and rushing mapwork.** Section B is precise, calculation-heavy work — don't arrive at it with five minutes and a tired hand.

---

## 5. YOUR 7-WEEK FOCUS (syncs with your Countdown Planner)

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full P1 AND P2 if you can, untimed — build your Gap Map. Note theory vs mapwork gaps |
| 2 | Mapwork calculations (both papers' Section B): drill all six until automatic — the highest-value, most predictable marks |
| 3 | P1 Climate & Weather: mid-latitude + tropical cyclones, synoptic map reading |
| 4 | P1 Geomorphology + one timed mapwork section: fluvial processes, drainage, slopes |
| 5 | P2 Settlement: rural & urban, site & situation, urban issues — then full timed Paper 1 |
| 6 | P2 Economic Geography of SA, then full Paper 2 simulation. Rework every lost mark |
| 7 | Taper: the six calculations from memory, synoptic symbols, your error list — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Audit first, then the mapwork calculations before any theory — 60 marks of identical, drillable method across the two papers is the fastest return on your time.

---

## 6. THE FINAL 48 HOURS

**DO:**
- Rework each of the six mapwork calculations once, from memory, with a real map
- Recite synoptic weather-map symbols and cyclone stages
- Redo your error list — especially unit and point-form slips
- Pack your kit: ruler, calculator (working batteries), pencil, protractor for bearings
- Sleep 8 hours — mapwork precision dies when you're tired

**DON'T:**
- Don't attempt a full new paper the night before
- Don't skip mapwork practice to reread theory — the calculations are the surest marks
- Don't answer paragraph questions in point form (the memo punishes it)
- Don't skip breakfast before a 3-hour paper
