## What an APS Is (60 Seconds)

Universities don't look at your marks the way your school does. They convert each subject percentage into an **achievement level from 1 to 7**, then add your levels together. That total is your **APS — Admission Point Score** — and it's the number that opens or closes course doors.

| Your % | Level (APS points) |
|---|---|
| 80 – 100% | 7 |
| 70 – 79% | 6 |
| 60 – 69% | 5 |
| 50 – 59% | 4 |
| 40 – 49% | 3 |
| 30 – 39% | 2 |
| 0 – 29% | 1 |

**Two things to check for YOUR situation:** universities differ on whether Life Orientation counts (many exclude it or count it differently), and some courses also demand minimum levels in specific subjects (e.g. "Maths level 5"). Always check the exact rules for your course on the university's own website or prospectus.

**Why this matters NOW, not in November:** your prelim marks are the marks universities see when they make provisional offers. The prelims aren't a practice run for your APS — for early admission, they largely *are* your APS.

## Step 1 — Find Your Target

Write down the course you actually want, and the APS it requires (from the university's website — not from a friend, not from memory):

> **Course:** ______________________________
>
> **University:** ______________________________
>
> **Required APS:** ________
>
> **Required subject levels (if any):** ______________________________

No course in mind yet? Pick the *highest* APS among the options you're considering — aiming at the tallest target keeps every door open.

## Step 3 — Find the Cheap Points

Here's the trick almost nobody teaches: **not all marks cost the same.** Moving from 62% to 68% earns you *zero* APS points. Moving from 68% to 70% earns you a *full point*. The cheapest APS points live wherever your marks sit just under a boundary.

Go down your table and circle every subject sitting within 5% of the next level (i.e. ending in 5–9: like 56%, 67%, 78%). Those are your **boundary subjects** — a few marks there buys a whole point.

Now cross-reference with your **Master Gap Map** from the planner: a boundary subject that's ALSO bleeding fixable marks is your golden target. That's where Weeks 2–3's double blocks earn double — marks AND points.

**Rules for sane targets:** a boundary subject can realistically jump 3–7% in seven weeks of focused repair. A subject needing a 15% jump is a two-target plan — one for prelims, one for finals. And never set a target that requires sacrificing two other subjects to feed one.

## Worked Example (So You See the Trick)

Thandi wants a course requiring **APS 34**. Her current report:

| Subject | % | Level |
|---|---|---|
| English HL | 68 | 5 |
| Afrikaans FAL | 71 | 6 |
| Maths Lit | 66 | 5 |
| Life Sciences | 58 | 4 |
| Geography | 63 | 5 |
| Business Studies | 77 | 6 |
| Life Orientation | 75 | 6 |

Counting her best 6 the way her target university does, she sits on **31. Gap: 3 points.**

The panic move: "study everything harder." The worksheet move: English (68) is 2% from a point. Business (77) is 3% from a point. Life Sciences (58) is 2% from a point. **Three boundary subjects, three cheap points, gap closed** — without touching Afrikaans, Maths Lit or Geography beyond maintenance. That's seven weeks aimed like a rifle instead of sprayed like a hosepipe.
