## The Paper at a Glance

Business Studies rewards the organised student over the clever one — the content is finite, the question formats repeat, and the marks are there for anyone who structures their answers properly. Two papers, four main topics.

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **Main topics** | Business Environments + Business Operations | Business Ventures + Business Roles |

**Both papers have the same three-section structure:**

| Section | What | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Compulsory — MCQ, choose-the-word, matching (short & objective) | 30 |
| **B** | Choose any 2 of 3 questions (40 each) | 80 |
| **C** | Choose any 1 of 2 essays (40 marks) | 40 |

## Where the Marks Live

**The cognitive split (every paper):**

| Low order (recall) | Middle order (application) | High order (evaluate/create) |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | 50% | 20% |

Half of every paper is **middle order** — "analyse, apply, discuss, explain, calculate." That's the zone to train hardest: not just knowing the content, but *doing* something with it in a scenario.

**The four main topics you're examined on:**

- **P1 → Business Environments:** recent legislation & its impact (the 8 Acts — BCEA, LRA, EEA, Skills Dev, BBBEE, NCA, CPA, COIDA), business strategies, business sectors
- **P1 → Business Operations:** human resources function, quality of performance (TQM)
- **P2 → Business Ventures:** management & leadership, investment (JSE, securities, insurance), forms of ownership, presentation & data response
- **P2 → Business Roles:** ethics & professionalism, creative thinking & problem-solving, CSR & CSI, human rights/inclusivity/diversity, team performance & conflict management

**The play:** Section C essays are worth 40 marks from a single question — the highest-value real estate in the paper. A learner who can write a strong essay in each paper has banked over a quarter of their marks on predictable, preparable topics. Essays are where organised students pull away.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## The Easy Marks

1. **Section A — 30 marks, compulsory, mostly recall.** MCQ, matching, choose-the-word. This is the friendliest 30 marks in the paper — drill past Section A's until they're quick and automatic.
2. **Definitions and "list/name/state" verbs.** Low-order action verbs ask you to recall, not reason. Keep a definitions list per topic from Week 1.
3. **The BBBEE pillars, the 8 Acts, the leadership styles** — these are *listable, memorisable* sets that come up almost every year. Learn the lists cold.
4. **Essay structure marks.** Section C essays award marks for introduction, body, conclusion and layout — you earn marks for the *shape* of the essay before the content is even judged. Learn the essay format.
5. **"Quote from the scenario" marks.** Case-study questions often ask you to identify something from the given scenario — the answer is in the text; you just have to spot and quote it correctly.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## The Killers

1. **Not answering the action verb.** "Discuss" ≠ "list." "Evaluate" needs positives AND negatives. "Recommend" needs a forward suggestion. Underline the verb; answer exactly what it demands.
2. **Essay with no structure.** A Section C essay without introduction/conclusion, or that ignores part of what's asked, forfeits the structure and insight marks even when the facts are right.
3. **One-word answers to paragraph questions.** Middle and higher-order marks need explanation. "Motivation improves productivity" earns one mark; explaining *how*, with the scenario, earns the rest.
4. **Running out of essay time.** A 40-mark essay plus Section B is a lot of writing in 2 hours. Budget your minutes and don't let a Section B question swallow your essay time.
5. **Ignoring the scenario/case study.** When a question gives a business scenario, your answer must *use* it — generic textbook answers that don't engage the case lose the application marks.
6. **Mixing up the topics per paper.** Don't walk into Paper 1 having only revised Ventures and Roles. Know which topics live in which paper.

## Your 7-Week Focus

Syncs with your Countdown Planner.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full P1 AND P2 if you can, untimed — build your Gap Map. Start definitions lists per topic |
| 2 | P1 Business Environments: the 8 Acts + BBBEE pillars (the biggest recall load in the subject) |
| 3 | P1 Business Operations (HR, quality) + drill Section A objective questions to speed |
| 4 | P2 Business Ventures: management & leadership styles/theories, investment & JSE — one timed section |
| 5 | P2 Business Roles: ethics, CSR/CSI, creative thinking, conflict — then learn the essay format cold |
| 6 | Essay drilling: write timed Section C essays for the most common topics, both papers. Full paper simulation |
| 7 | Taper: definitions lists, the Acts, essay skeletons from memory, your error list — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Audit first, then Business Environments (heaviest recall) and the essay format — essays are 40 predictable marks you can prepare in advance.

## The Final 48 Hours

**DO**

- Recite your definitions lists + the 8 Acts + BBBEE pillars out loud
- Rewrite your essay skeletons (intro/body/conclusion) for the likely Section C topics
- One timed Section A for speed and confidence
- Redo your error list — especially action-verb slips
- Sleep 8 hours — clear thinking beats cramped cramming for essay writing

**DON'T**

- Don't attempt a full new paper the night before
- Don't neglect essay practice to reread notes — essays are where the marks move
- Don't revise only one paper's topics
- Don't skip breakfast before a paper
