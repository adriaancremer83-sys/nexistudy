## The Paper at a Glance

History rewards the student who can *argue with evidence*, not just remember dates. Two skills carry the whole subject: reading sources and building essays — and both can be trained like any other skill. Two papers, written on separate days.

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **Section A** | Source-based questions (3 × 50) | Source-based questions (3 × 50) |
| **Section B** | Essay questions (3 × 50) | Essay questions (3 × 50) |

**How you answer (both papers):** You answer **THREE questions of 50 marks each**. At least ONE must be source-based, at least ONE must be an essay; the third is your choice. **Spend about one hour per question.** You choose which topics to answer — so you can play to your strengths, but you must prepare enough topics to have real choice.

**The content** spans apartheid-era resistance, the coming of democracy and the TRC, the Cold War, independent Africa and the new world order — split across the two papers by era.

## Where the Marks Live

Every question is worth 50 marks, and there are only two types:

- **Source-based (50):** a set of sub-questions on 4 sources, building from simple extraction ("quote from the source") up to comparison and reliability, ending with an 8-mark paragraph.
- **Essay (50):** one extended argument, about three pages, defending a line of argument with historical evidence.

**The play:** Because you choose your three questions and can lean source-based OR essay, your prep strategy is everything. Source questions reward *technique* (which you can drill on any topic); essays reward *prepared content* (which you must build in advance). Most students should master source technique cold — it's transferable across every topic — then deeply prepare 2–3 essay topics they can write in their sleep. That combination guarantees you always have three strong questions to choose from.

## The Easy Marks

1. **"Quote from the source" and "list/name" questions.** The opening sub-questions (1 mark each) ask you to lift the answer straight from the source. Read carefully, copy exactly, get them all. These are free.
2. **"Define the term in your own words."** Concepts like *globalisation, traitor, rent boycott, protectionist* — 2 marks for a clear definition. Keep a concepts list per topic.
3. **The 8-mark paragraph.** The final source question asks for ~80 words using the sources + your own knowledge. It has a clear structure — a few relevant points, each developed — and it's very preparable once you know the format.
4. **Essay structure marks.** A History essay earns marks for a clear introduction (with a line of argument), well-organised body paragraphs, and a conclusion. You get rewarded for the *shape* before the content is even weighed. Learn the essay format.
5. **"Own knowledge" additions.** Many source questions say "using the source AND your own knowledge." One extra relevant fact you bring earns the second mark. Reward for what you revised.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## The Killers

1. **Rewriting the source as your answer.** The paper explicitly warns: you'll be *disadvantaged by merely rewriting the sources*. Higher-mark source questions want interpretation, comparison and your own knowledge — not copied lines.
2. **Essay with no line of argument.** "Do you agree?" and "Critically discuss" demand a *position* defended throughout. A narrative that just retells events, without arguing, caps your marks well below the top.
3. **Ignoring the question's command word.** "To what extent," "critically discuss," "do you agree" each demand a specific kind of answer. Underline it; structure your essay around answering exactly that.
4. **Time mismanagement.** Three 50-mark questions in 3 hours = one hour each, no exceptions. A brilliant two-question paper still fails if you never wrote the third. Watch the clock ruthlessly.
5. **Thin evidence in essays.** "Support your line of argument with relevant historical evidence" means names, dates, events, organisations — specifics. A passionate argument with no evidence is an opinion, not history.
6. **Not answering the reliability/usefulness questions properly.** "Why would a historian regard this source as useful/reliable?" needs comment on the source's *origin and nature*, not just its content.

## Your 7-Week Focus

Syncs with your Countdown Planner.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full source-based question AND one essay, untimed — build your Gap Map. Start a concepts list |
| 2 | Source technique: drill every sub-question type (quote, list, define, compare, reliability, the 8-mark paragraph) across different topics — this transfers everywhere |
| 3 | Essay topic 1 (choose a strong one): build a full plan + write it timed. Learn the intro/argument/conclusion structure |
| 4 | Essay topic 2 + one timed source question: same deep prep, different topic |
| 5 | Essay topic 3 + full timed Paper 1: now you have real choice on exam day |
| 6 | Full Paper 2 simulation under exam conditions (1 hour per question). Rework every lost mark |
| 7 | Taper: concepts list, essay plans from memory, source-technique checklist — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Drill source technique first — it works on every topic and is the fastest route to reliable marks. Then prepare your two strongest essay topics so you can always pick a source question + an essay you know cold.

## The Final 48 Hours

**DO**

- Review your prepared essay plans — intro line of argument, body points, conclusion
- Run your source-technique checklist (how to answer quote / define / compare / reliability / the paragraph)
- Recite your concepts list per topic
- Redo your error list — especially "rewriting the source" and "no argument" slips
- Sleep 8 hours — clear argument-building needs a rested brain

**DON'T**

- Don't try to learn a whole new topic the night before
- Don't neglect source technique to only memorise content — technique is half the paper
- Don't plan to answer only two questions "really well" — three at one hour each beats two perfect ones
- Don't skip breakfast before a 3-hour paper
