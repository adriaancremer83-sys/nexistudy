## The Paper at a Glance

You write two papers. Same length, same marks — but they cover different halves of the subject.

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **The territory** | Money & numbers: Finance (60%), Data handling (35%) | Space & shape: Measurement (55%), Maps & plans (40%) |
| **Question 1** | ±30 marks, easiest level | ±30 marks, easiest level |

Don't believe anyone who tells you Paper 1 is the easy one and Paper 2 the hard one — that stopped being true in 2021. The difference is **territory, not difficulty**. Probability sneaks its ±5% into both papers inside other questions, and a Finance question can appear in Paper 2 when it links to plans or measurement (like costing a building project).

> Question 1 in EACH paper is a ±30-mark warm-up at the easiest level — matching definitions, reading values off a graph. That is roughly 60 marks across the two papers waiting for you before the real work starts. Bank them.

## Where the Marks Live

Across both papers combined, the syllabus weights the five topics like this:

| Topic | Weight | Where it hits hardest |
|---|---|---|
| **Finance** | ±35% | Interest, tariffs, VAT, till slips, budgets, exchange rates |
| **Data handling** | ±25% | Tables, graphs, mean/median/mode, quartiles |
| **Measurement** | ±20% | Conversions, area, volume, packaging |
| **Maps, plans & representations** | ±15% | Scale, floor plans, directions |
| **Probability** | ±5% | Weather, games, tree diagrams |

**The play:** Finance and Data together are worth about 60% of your total mark — and they are basically the whole of Paper 1. If your interest calculations, tariff tables and mean/median work are solid, you have secured one entire paper before you touch a floor plan. Master those two first, every week. Probability is worth the least — don't give it your best hours, but don't skip it either: its 5% is some of the easiest 5% in the paper.

## The Easy Marks

They're handed to you — learners throw them away every year.

1. **Reading values straight off a table or graph.** No calculation. The answer is printed on the page — find it, copy it correctly, with units.
2. **Substituting into a formula they give you.** The formula is in the question. Replace the letters with the numbers, one step at a time, and show it.
3. **Rounding and units.** Final answers rounded as instructed, with the right unit written down, collect marks on almost every question.
4. **"Show that..." questions.** The answer is already printed in the question. You only have to show the steps that lead there.
5. **Working shown = marks protected.** Markers award method marks and follow your working — a correct method can still earn marks after an arithmetic slip, but only if the working is on the page. A bare final number risks everything on one digit.

> If you write down working for every question — even ones you're unsure of — you are already ahead of half the province.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## The Killers

The mistakes markers punish year after year.

1. **Rounding at the wrong moment.** Round only at the end, and round the way the context demands: 4.2 buses means you need **5** buses. 4.8 full tins of paint from a budget means you can afford **4**. The context decides, not the decimal.
2. **Unit blindness.** Mixing mm with cm, ml with litres, grams with kilograms — one missed conversion poisons every mark that follows. Write the units in every line of working.
3. **Treating time like a decimal.** 2.5 hours is 2 hours 30 minutes, not 2 hours 50. Time calculations look easy — which is exactly why they catch you.
4. **Answering the wrong question.** The scenario asks for cost per *month* and you give per *year*. Underline what is actually being asked before you calculate anything.
5. **No working shown.** In Maths Lit, working IS the answer. A bare final number can score zero even when it's close — and forfeits every method mark when it's wrong.

## Your 7-Week Focus

Synced to the Countdown Planner — depth beats coverage.

| Week | Dates | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13 – 19 July | **Finance I:** interest (simple & compound), VAT, tariff tables & till slips |
| 2 | 20 – 26 July | **Finance II:** budgets, exchange rates, banking costs — then a timed finance section from a past paper |
| 3 | 27&nbsp;Jul&nbsp;–&nbsp;2&nbsp;Aug | **Data handling:** mean/median/mode, quartiles, reading & drawing graphs |
| 4 | 3 – 9 Aug | **Measurement:** conversions, perimeter/area/volume — drill unit discipline |
| 5 | 10 – 16 Aug | **Maps & plans + probability:** scale, floor plans, tree diagrams |
| 6 | 17 – 23 Aug | **Full Paper 1 under exam conditions.** Mark it with the memo. Rework every lost mark |

**Starting late?** Don't try to catch up week by week — jump straight to the current week and steal extra time for Finance and Data first. Sixty percent of the marks forgives a lot.

## The Final 48 Hours

**DO**

- Redo your personal error list — the questions *you* got wrong, not new ones
- Work through one tariff table and one interest calculation to stay sharp
- Pack your calculator (working batteries), ruler and pens the night before
- Sleep. A rested brain reads scenarios properly; a tired one misreads them

**DON'T**

- Don't attempt a full new past paper the night before — it can only cost confidence
- Don't cram complicated formulas at midnight — they are given in the question. Just make sure perimeter, area and volume are second nature
- Don't compare notes with panicking friends outside the exam room
- Don't skip breakfast — three hours of reading and calculating burns real energy
