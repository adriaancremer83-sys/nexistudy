## The Paper at a Glance

Accounting is one of the few subjects where you can genuinely target a distinction — the marks are logical, repetitive, and reward preparation over cleverness. Since 2020 it's split into two papers, each its own discipline.

| | Paper 1 — Financial Reporting | Paper 2 — Managerial Accounting |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **The territory** | Companies: ledgers, Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, ratio analysis, asset valuation, corporate governance | Reconciliations, VAT, inventory valuation, manufacturing/cost accounting, cash budgets, projected income statements, internal control & audit |

Every question is **compulsory** and answered in a **specially prepared answer book** — but the answer book doesn't repeat the full question, so always read the question paper too. The structure is given; your job is the right figures in the right places.

## How the Marks Are Built

Accounting doesn't publish topic percentages — instead every paper is built to a **degree-of-challenge split**:

| Easy | Moderate | Difficult |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | 40% | 30% |

**What this means for you:** a full 30% of every paper — 45 marks — is deliberately set at Easy level. That's format marks, concepts, straightforward calculations. Bank all of it before you spend a second fighting the Difficult 30%. Most learners lose distinctions not on the hard marks, but by leaving easy ones behind in a panic.

**Paper 1's big blocks:** Company financial statements (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow) and ratio analysis & interpretation are the two dominant, most predictable questions — the format barely changes year to year.

**Paper 2's big blocks:** Manufacturing/cost accounting and cash budgets, plus reconciliations and VAT. Internal control and ethics thread through both.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## The Easy Marks

1. **Format marks.** Statements and ledgers earn marks for correct layout, headings and structure — before the figures are even right. Learn the skeletons cold; you get paid for the shape.
2. **Concept & ethics questions.** GAAP/IFRS principles, internal control, corporate governance, code of conduct — short, memorisable, and they open most questions. Keep a concepts list from Week 1.
3. **Ratio & indicator formulas.** Each financial indicator is a formula applied to given figures. Learn the list (both papers have their own indicator set) and the calculation marks are yours regardless of your interpretation.
4. **Own-figure / method marks.** Accounting marking follows your figures forward — a wrong subtotal carried correctly into the next line still earns later marks, *if* your method is visible. Show every calculation in brackets.
5. **Balancing and bringing down in ledgers.** The mechanical steps are marks awarded for method, not insight.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## The Killers

1. **Answering without workings.** In calculation questions, the workings in brackets earn method marks even when the final figure is wrong. A bare number risks everything.
2. **Wrong side of the ledger.** Debit vs credit errors cascade — one flipped side can unravel a whole account. Slow down on entry direction.
3. **Dropping the interpretation sentences.** Ratio and cash-flow questions ask you to *comment and quote figures* — "is liquidity healthy? quote the ratio." A calculation with no comment, or a comment with no figures, leaves easy marks behind.
4. **Time management — and it's tighter now.** Each paper is 2 hours for 150 marks. Budget roughly a minute per mark and move on. An unfinished ratio question you could've aced hurts more than a slightly rushed statement.
5. **Ignoring the adjustments.** The "additional information" notes ARE the question — depreciation, accruals, prepaid, bad debts. Miss one and every figure below it is wrong.
6. **Messy, unlabelled figures.** The marker must find your answer in the answer book. Wrong line, no label, illegible = a mark they can't award.

## Your 7-Week Focus

Syncs with your Countdown Planner.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full P1 AND one full P2 if you can, untimed — build your Gap Map. Start your concepts list |
| 2 | P1 Companies I: ledgers, Income Statement, Balance Sheet layout — drill the skeletons until automatic |
| 3 | P1 Companies II: notes + Cash Flow Statement, then ratio analysis (learn every P1 indicator + "quote & comment") |
| 4 | P2 Manufacturing + cost accounting: production cost statement, unit costs, break-even — one timed section |
| 5 | P2 Budgets + reconciliations + VAT + inventory valuation, then a full timed Paper 2 |
| 6 | Full Paper 1 simulation under exam conditions; rework every lost mark. Internal control + ethics polish |
| 7 | Taper: concepts list, statement skeletons + both indicator lists from memory, your error list — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Audit first, then P1 statements + ratios and P2 manufacturing — the biggest, most predictable blocks. Format skeletons every week; they're free marks that compound.

## The Final 48 Hours

**DO**

- Rewrite the statement skeletons (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow) from memory
- Recite your concepts list + both papers' financial-indicator formulas out loud
- Redo your error list — especially adjustment and debit/credit slips
- One timed ratio-interpretation question for the "quote figures + comment" rhythm
- Sleep 8 hours — Accounting rewards a calm, methodical brain

**DON'T**

- Don't attempt a full new paper the night before
- Don't leave the interpretation comments for last and run out of time (2 hours is tight)
- Don't chase the Difficult 30% while easy format marks sit unclaimed
- Don't skip breakfast before the paper
