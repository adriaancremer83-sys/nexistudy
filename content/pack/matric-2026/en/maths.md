## The Paper at a Glance

Two papers, two completely different worlds. Learners who prepare for "Maths" instead of for "Paper 1" and "Paper 2" separately get ambushed in September.

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| **Marks** | 150 | 150 |
| **The territory** | Algebra & equations, patterns & sequences, functions & graphs, finance, calculus, probability | Statistics & regression, analytical geometry, trigonometry, Euclidean geometry |

An **information sheet with formulas is included in the exam** — but it gives you the formula, not the knowledge of *when* to use it. That part is trained, not given.

## Where the Marks Live

### Paper 1 (150 marks)

| Topic | Marks |
|---|---|
| Functions & graphs | 35 |
| Differential calculus | 35 |
| Algebra, equations & inequalities | 25 |
| Number patterns | 25 |
| Finance, growth & decay | 15 |
| Counting principle & probability | 15 |

### Paper 2 (150 marks)

| Topic | Marks |
|---|---|
| Trigonometry | 50 |
| Analytical geometry | 40 |
| Euclidean geometry | 40 |
| Statistics & regression | 20 |

**The play:** Functions + Calculus = 70 of Paper 1's 150 marks, and they feed each other — a calculus question usually lives on a graph. Trigonometry alone is a **third of Paper 2**. Master Functions, Calculus and Trig and you've claimed the three biggest territories in the subject. And Question 1 of Paper 1 is almost always straight algebra — solving equations and inequalities — the friendliest marks in either paper.

## The Easy Marks

1. **Paper 1, Question 1.** Factorise, quadratic formula, inequalities, exponents — the same families of equations every single year. Drill them until they're reflexes; this is the calmest start an exam can give you.
2. **"Write down..." questions.** When a question says *write down*, no working is expected — the answer is one step away (an asymptote, a y-intercept, the next term). Don't manufacture a calculation.
3. **First sub-questions everywhere.** The (a) parts — plot the point, state the gradient, find the mean — are level-one marks even inside hard questions. Never abandon a question before reading part (a).
4. **Statistics on your calculator.** Mean, standard deviation, regression — your calculator does these in seconds if you've learned the buttons *before* exam day.
5. **The formulas are on the information sheet.** The quadratic formula, trig identities, area rules — provided. Marks for substitution are the cheapest in the paper: write the formula, substitute visibly, then solve.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## The Killers

1. **No working, no marks.** Maths marking rewards method. An answer straight from the calculator with no steps can score a fraction of the marks even when correct — and zero when it's wrong. Show every step, even "obvious" ones.
2. **Geometry without reasons.** In Euclidean geometry, every statement needs its reason ("angles in same segment", "tan-chord"). A perfect proof with missing reasons bleeds half its marks.
3. **Rounding and the ± sign.** Round only at the end, to what the instructions demand (usually two decimal places unless the question says otherwise) — and never forget ± when square-rooting an equation. That missing minus is a classic lost mark.
4. **Calculator in the wrong mode.** Trig answers that look "almost right" usually mean the calculator is in radians. Check DEGREE mode before the paper starts, not during Question 5.
5. **Sketching graphs without the furniture.** Intercepts, turning points, asymptotes — each is a mark. A beautiful curve with no labels is worth almost nothing.
6. **Skipping the interpretation sentence.** Calculus and finance questions often end with "interpret your answer." One plain-language sentence = one mark. Learners leave it blank because it feels too easy. It isn't a trick.

## Your 7-Week Focus

Syncs with your Countdown Planner.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit: one full past paper (start with P1), untimed — build your Gap Map. Note which paper's territory bleeds worse |
| 2 | Algebra + Functions & graphs: the foundation everything else stands on. Drill P1 Q1-style algebra daily as a warm-up |
| 3 | Calculus + Finance: rules of differentiation, cubic graphs, optimisation; then annuities & depreciation |
| 4 | Trigonometry: identities, general solutions, 2D/3D problems — plus one timed P1 section this week |
| 5 | Analytical + Euclidean geometry: circle theorems WITH reasons, then full timed Paper 1 |
| 6 | Full Paper 2 simulation under exam conditions; rework every lost mark. Statistics polish (calculator drills) |
| 7 | Taper: your error list, information-sheet fluency, the prescribed theorem proofs (listed in the DBE exam guideline) — nothing new after Tuesday |

**Starting late?** Audit first, always. Then let your Gap Map choose between the Week 2–3 blocks — but protect the daily Q1 algebra warm-up; it's free marks compounding.

## The Final 48 Hours

**DO**

- Redo your error list — especially the algebra slips and geometry reasons
- One timed P1 Question 1 for confidence (10 minutes, done)
- Rewrite the prescribed theorem proofs once, from memory
- Check calculator: DEGREE mode, working batteries, approved model
- Sleep 8 hours — accuracy dies faster than knowledge when you're tired

**DON'T**

- Don't attempt full new papers the night before
- Don't memorise the information sheet — it's given to you; know how to *use* it
- Don't practise only your favourite topic (it's Functions. It's always Functions.)
- Don't skip breakfast before a 3-hour paper
